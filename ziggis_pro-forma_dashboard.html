Rent % iPercentage rent. Rent equals this percentage of the selected sales basis, payable monthly. Gross: all sales before discounts — the larger base, favors the landlord. Net: gross sales less discounts and comps — the smaller base, favors the tenant. Because rent moves with revenue, the Bear/Base/Bull scenarios and multi-year projections flex rent automatically.  ✎
Additional rent (fixed $/yr) iFixed additional rent. Any flat amount owed on top of percentage rent. Enter the annual total — e.g. $5,000 per quarter is $20,000 per year. ✎
NNN (taxes, insurance, CAM) iTriple net charges — typically fixed pass-throughs, not negotiable. Property taxes, building insurance, and common area maintenance billed by the landlord. Enter the annual total; leave $0 if your lease has no NNN (e.g. a license or percentage-only deal) or if you own the property. ✎

How to generate your SBA-compliant pro-formaNew to this? Just follow the steps
  1. Set your operating assumptions. In the left sidebar, enter your average daily revenue, discount %, COGS %, labor %, and your fixed costs. These drive the projection.
  2. Set up your loan. Open the Loan Details tab and enter your project total, loan structure (7(a) current, 7(a) new rules, or 504), interest rate, and term. This builds your amortization and debt service.
  3. Fill in the items below. These are the startup costs and expenses an SBA lender expects but that aren't part of the day-to-day model: your Uses of Funds (from FDD Item 7), how you're funding your equity injection, your owner's salary, and a few operating costs. Leave anything that doesn't apply at $0.
  4. Generate the workbook. Click Generate SBA Excel at the bottom. You'll get a five-sheet file — Sources & Uses, Loan Amortization, and monthly Year 1, 2, and 3 statements — with your Debt Service Coverage Ratio and a stress test.
What lenders look for: the key number is your Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) — operating income divided by loan payments. SBA generally wants this at 1.15x or higher within the first two years. Always have the finished file reviewed by your lender and a qualified advisor before relying on it.
